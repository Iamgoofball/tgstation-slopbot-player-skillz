# Admin conduct guide (tgstation)

What the server expects of an admin acting in an official capacity, in and out of the game. **No crew member's briefing includes this guide.** The crew have no admin powers (the standing orders say so), and nothing in `agents/` or `crew-basics.md` points here. It is for whoever is given admin rights on a server, and for judging whether an admin's action was fair. A player who thinks an admin broke these rules adminhelps the complaint (see `server-rules.md`, section 6, for the forums route).

This guide follows the /tg/station wiki's Admin Conduct page. It isn't checked against the server's code.

## 1. Admin rules

- **Follow the headmins' directives.** They're posted on the admin forum (https://tgstation13.org/phpBB/viewtopic.php?f=29&t=9987).
- **Be in IRC or Discord while adminning** on the servers.
- **Don't cheat.** Never use admin powers for your own benefit or against players.
- **Don't handle an adminhelp that involves you.** You can't act as both admin and player in one matter. To report an issue, adminhelp as any player would, and another admin will take it.
- **Don't interfere with another admin's ban or adminhelp unless asked.** Pointing out a missed detail or useful information is fine. Higher-ranked admins are encouraged to advise, but overruling someone's ban is for the headmins.
- **Keep privacy.** Never publish IPs or computer IDs, in the game or outside it.
- **Don't be a hypocrite.** If you're doing what you'd ban someone for, you're breaking this rule.
- **Don't spam sounds and events.** Queuing sounds back to back, or running an event every day, is too much.
- **Don't apply bans longer than an hour.**

## 2. General guidelines

- If you're unsure, ask the other admins in the game and on adminbus. If they disagree a lot, ask a Head Admin.
- Stay professional in the game, on the forums and in other Space Station 13 communities. You can still chat casually with players.
- **Be careful when you test something you don't understand.** A quick check (how much damage does a stun baton do?) is fine. An outlandish one, such as var editing, belongs on your own local server, not the live one: you can crash it.
- **De-admin while you play a sensitive role** (security, the Captain, the AI). You avoid the flood of metainfo, and players don't suspect you of cheating, which has really happened.
- If you opened a ticket during a round, **don't readmin until it's resolved.** If a ticket was opened about you, discuss it only in adminhelps.
- Using admin buttons to mess with friends or random players is a gamble on admin complaints, and you do it at your own risk. New admins who pick up bad habits from tenured ones may hold those tenured admins accountable. Respect players' boundaries and answer for what you cause.

## 3. Events and badminning

- Before pressing any button, weigh the population, the time in the round, how many people are AFK or dead, and whether people are enjoying the round or busy on projects.
- **Don't interfere with or add your own spice to another admin's event.**
- To fill a role (an event character, someone AFK), **prefer players to admins.** The admins are there to make fun for the players. If you play an antagonist event character yourself, think twice.
- **Don't stack sound files or split a long song into pieces to play it.** It slows the game for players abroad and on bad connections.
- A good test: would you have found it fine as a player?
- Above all, keep the game fun and fair for the players.

## 4. Notes and bans

- Don't change non-secret notes or bans outside an appeal, unless the change benefits the player, or you're fixing an error within a reasonable time of placing it.
- **Each new incident gets a new note.** Don't edit an old one.
- If you're unsure about editing a note, or the case falls outside this, ask the head admin team.

## 5. In the client

Admin verbs run by name (`command {line: "Player-Panel"}`); how they prompt, and how to use them through the tools, is in the seeker client's `tgstation-client` skill. That skill describes a developer's test server, where an agent is the developer's assistant and these conduct rules mostly don't apply. On a server with real players they do: the headmin directives, privacy, the one-hour ban cap and the rules on notes all hold.
